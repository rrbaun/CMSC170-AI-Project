PROJECT STATEMENT & MOTIVATION

Exploring patterns
within data.

Public health planning relies on understanding how mortality burdens vary across geographic regions, age brackets, and biological sex. Raw national vital statistics tables break deaths down across dozens of demographic cross-tabulations per cause of death, making it difficult to manually identify shared regional disease profiles or systematically flag high-impact demographic-cause segments.

Objective

To evaluate unsupervised clustering (K-Means) and supervised classification (KNN and Perceptron) on the 2024 Philippine regional mortality dataset—analyzing regional mortality distribution patterns and testing public health risk tier classification (High vs. Low Mortality Impact) across age group, sex, and cause of death—to determine which method(s) yield a helpful, well-supported conclusion about the data.

DATASET DESCRIPTION

Dataset: Number of Registered Deaths by Age Group, Sex, Region of Usual Residence and Cause of Death, Philippines: 2024

  • Source: Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) OpenSTAT — Population and Vital Statistics
  • Geographic Coverage: Eight selected Philippine regions (NCR, CAR, Region I, Region II, Region III, Region IV-A, Region IV-B, and Region V)
  • Raw Dimensions: 928 rows x 65 columns
  • Cleaned Dimensions: 920 rows (737 training / 183 testing in the 80:20 split) x [TODO: exact cleaned column count] columns
  • Features & Target: Each observation represents a specific region and single cause of death, with numerical features consisting of male and female mortality counts broken down across individual age brackets (e.g., Under 1, 1-4, 5-9, …, 85 and over). For supervised tasks, observations are labeled as High Mortality Impact or Low Mortality Impact.

ALGORITHM 01

K-Means

How it works:

K-Means partitions unlabeled observations into k disjoint clusters by iteratively assigning each data point to its nearest centroid (via Euclidean distance) and recomputing each centroid as the mean of its assigned points until cluster assignments converge.


Implementation & Role:

Implemented from scratch with iterative centroid updates (converging at iteration 19 for k = 6) to uncover natural geographic and life-stage mortality groupings across regions and causes of death.

ALGORITHM 02

K-Nearest Neighbors

How it works:

KNN is a non-parametric, instance-based classifier that computes distances between a query observation and all training observations, predicting the majority class label among its k closest neighbors in feature space.


Implementation & Role:

Implemented from scratch with Euclidean distance calculation and majority voting across k in {3, 5, 7, 9} to capture localized, non-linear boundaries between High and Low mortality segments.

ALGORITHM 03

Perceptron

How it works:

The Perceptron learns a linear decision boundary by iteratively updating weights and bias whenever a training instance is misclassified, converging to zero errors only if the classes are linearly separable.


Implementation & Role:

Implemented from scratch as a linear baseline to test whether demographic age-and-sex feature combinations allow a single hyperplane to separate High and Low mortality risk tiers.

EXPERIMENTAL SETUP

Data Split:

For supervised models (KNN and Perceptron), the 920 cleaned records were partitioned using an 80:20 stratified train-test split (737 training observations, 183 testing observations) to preserve class proportions across High and Low Mortality Impact categories. Because K-Means is unsupervised, it was evaluated directly on the unlabeled feature matrix without a train-test split.

  • Hyperparameters Tested:


  • K-Means: Evaluated across candidate cluster counts k (with k = 6 highlighted in Milestone 2; max iterations until convergence at iteration 19).
  • KNN: Tested k in {3, 5, 7, 9} using Euclidean distance.
  • Perceptron: Learning rate eta = 0.1, trained for up to 100 epochs.

    • Evaluation Metrics (Implemented From Scratch):

  • Silhouette Score (K-Means): Measures intra-cluster cohesion versus nearest-cluster separation ([-1, 1]) without requiring ground-truth labels.
  • Accuracy, Precision, Recall & Macro F1-Score (KNN & Perceptron): Macro F1 weights High and Low mortality classes equally so majority-class accuracy does not mask minority-class errors.
  • Confusion Matrix (KNN & Perceptron): Breaks down True Positives, True Negatives, False Positives, and False Negatives across risk categories.
