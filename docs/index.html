01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Finding structure in complex data.

WHY THIS PROJECT?

Making mortality patterns easier to examine.

Public health planning relies on understanding how mortality burdens vary across geographic regions, age brackets, and biological sex. National vital statistics contain many demographic cross-tabulations, making shared regional patterns and high-impact segments difficult to identify manually.

PROJECT OBJECTIVE
↗

Evaluate K-Means, KNN, and Perceptron on the 2024 Philippine regional mortality dataset to analyze regional mortality patterns and classify High vs. Low Mortality Impact across age group, sex, and cause of death.

FEATURES & TARGET

What does one observation represent?

Each observation represents a specific region and cause of death. Numerical features contain male and female mortality counts across age brackets, from Under 1 through 85 and over. For supervised tasks, observations are labeled High Mortality Impact or Low Mortality Impact.

DATA PREPARATION

Preprocessing pipeline

01 Data Loading Parsed raw CSV records using Python's built-in csv module and converted numerical feature matrices into NumPy arrays
02 Data Cleaning Standardized region names and cause-of-death labels, validated numerical entries, and retained zero mortality counts as valid epidemiological observations.
03 Category & Row/Column Filtering Audited cause-of-death hierarchies to identify overlapping parent and subcategories, removing redundant aggregate rows (reducing 928 rows to 920 rows).
04 Feature Selection & Labeling Extracted age-group and sex-specific mortality count columns as numerical features for K-Means, and paired them with High/Low mortality tier labels for KNN and Perceptron.

01

K-Means

Discovering groups within unlabeled observations.

HOW IT WORKS

K-Means partitions observations into k clusters. Each point is assigned to its nearest centroid using Euclidean distance, then centroids are recalculated as the mean of their assigned points until assignments converge.

IMPLEMENTATION & ROLE

Implemented from scratch with iterative centroid updates. The run highlighted in the project converged at iteration 58 for k = 12, to examine geographic and life-stage mortality groupings across regions and causes of death.

02

K-Nearest Neighbors

Classifying records through their nearest neighbors.

HOW IT WORKS

KNN is an instance-based classifier. It calculates the distance between a query observation and training observations, then predicts the majority class among its k closest neighbors.

IMPLEMENTATION & ROLE

Implemented from scratch using Euclidean distance and majority voting across k values of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, and 21 to classify High and Low mortality segments.

03

Perceptron

Testing whether a linear boundary can separate the classes.

HOW IT WORKS

The Perceptron learns a linear decision boundary by updating its weights and bias whenever a training instance is misclassified. It reaches zero training errors only when the classes are linearly separable.

IMPLEMENTATION & ROLE

Implemented from scratch as a linear baseline to test whether demographic age-and-sex feature combinations can separate High and Low mortality risk tiers using a single hyperplane.

05 / RESULTS & DISCUSSION

What did the models reveal?

Compare model performance and interpret what the results suggest about the dataset.

WHY k = 12 IN K-MEANS?

Balancing Mathematical Separation & Public Health Granularity

Although k = 2 (0.9495) and k = 3 (0.8626) yield higher global Silhouette Scores, they collapse distinct epidemiological realities into two or three overly broad buckets—merging urban lifestyle diseases with general provincial baselines and obscuring demographic nuances. At k = 12 (0.6992), the model reaches a clear local peak right before over-fragmentation causes scores to drop sharply to 0.5448 at k = 13 and plateau below 0.56 up to k = 25. This configuration cleanly isolates breast cancer in middle-aged women (Cluster 10), young male transport trauma (Cluster 8), and perinatal vs. congenital infant mortality (Clusters 2 & 9).

KNN VS. PERCEPTRON ANALYSIS

Non-Linear Localized Structure vs. Linear Hyperplane

In supervised classification, KNN (k = 5) achieved a Macro F1-Score of 0.9836 (180/183 correct predictions), classifying all 91 Low Mortality Impact observations accurately while misclassifying only 3 High Mortality Impact observations. In contrast, the Perceptron plateaued at 65% test accuracy (Macro F1 = 0.6170, with a minimum of 212 training errors across epochs) and never converged to zero errors. This confirms that High vs. Low mortality segments are not linearly separable by a single hyperplane—spanning multiple distinct age-sex regimes (such as infant perinatal peaks vs. senior cardiovascular peaks) that localized neighborhood voting (KNN) and localized clustering (K-Means) capture effectively.

PROJECT CONCLUSION

Different algorithms reveal different kinds of structure.

Applying K-Means, KNN, and Perceptron to the 2024 PSA Philippine mortality dataset showed distinct patterns in model behavior. K-Means (Silhouette Score = 0.6992) identified epidemiological subgroups, including infant mortality and NCR-concentrated chronic diseases. KNN (k = 5, Macro F1 = 0.9836) classified High and Low mortality risk tiers with high performance. The Perceptron (Macro F1 = 0.6170) indicated that the profiles were not well separated by a single linear boundary.
