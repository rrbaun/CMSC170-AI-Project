1. Problem Statement and Motivation

Public health planning relies on understanding how mortality burdens vary across geographic regions, age brackets, and biological sex. Raw national vital statistics tables break deaths down across dozens of demographic cross-tabulations per cause of death, making it difficult to manually identify shared regional disease profiles or systematically flag high-impact demographic-cause segments.

Objective: To evaluate unsupervised clustering (K-Means) and supervised classification (KNN and Perceptron) on the 2024 Philippine regional mortality dataset—analyzing regional mortality distribution patterns and testing public health risk tier classification (High vs. Low Mortality Impact) across age group, sex, and cause of death—to determine which method(s) yield a helpful, well-supported conclusion about the data.

2. Dataset Description

  • Dataset: Number of Registered Deaths by Age Group, Sex, Region of Usual Residence and Cause of Death, Philippines: 2024
  • Source: Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) OpenSTAT — Population and Vital Statistics
  • Geographic Coverage: Eight selected Philippine regions (NCR, CAR, Region I, Region II, Region III, Region IV-A, Region IV-B, and Region V)
  • Raw Dimensions: 928 rows × 65 columns
  • Cleaned Dimensions: 920 rows (737 training / 183 testing in the 80:20 split) × [TODO: exact cleaned column count] columns
  • Features & Target: Each observation represents a specific region and single cause of death, with numerical features consisting of male and female mortality counts broken down across individual age brackets (e.g., Under 1, 1–4, 5–9, …, 85 and over). For supervised tasks, observations are labeled as High Mortality Impact or Low Mortality Impact.

Preprocessing Pipeline (Implemented From Scratch)

  1. Data Loading: Parsed raw CSV records using Python's built-in csv module and converted numerical feature matrices into NumPy arrays without using pandas.
  2. Data Cleaning: Standardized region names and cause-of-death labels, validated numerical entries, and retained zero mortality counts as valid epidemiological observations.
  3. Category Verification & Row/Column Filtering: Audited cause-of-death hierarchies to identify overlapping parent and subcategories, removing redundant aggregate rows (reducing 928 rows to 920 rows).
  4. Feature Selection & Labeling: Extracted age-group and sex-specific mortality count columns as numerical features for K-Means, and paired them with High/Low mortality tier labels for KNN and Perceptron.

3. Methods (From-Scratch Implementations)

All three methods were implemented from scratch using only Python's standard library and NumPy for array and basic linear algebra operations:

A. K-Means Clustering

How It Works: K-Means partitions unlabeled observations into k disjoint clusters by iteratively assigning each data point to its nearest centroid (via Euclidean distance) and recomputing each centroid as the mean of its assigned points until cluster assignments converge.

Implementation & Role: Implemented from scratch with iterative centroid updates (converging at iteration 19 for k = 6) to uncover natural geographic and life-stage mortality groupings across regions and causes of death.

B. K-Nearest Neighbors — KNN (Supervised Non-Linear Classifier)

How It Works: KNN is a non-parametric, instance-based classifier that computes distances between a query observation and all training observations, predicting the majority class label among its k closest neighbors in feature space.

Implementation & Role: Implemented from scratch with Euclidean distance calculation and majority voting across k in {3, 5, 7, 9} to capture localized, non-linear boundaries between High and Low mortality segments.

C. Perceptron (Supervised Linear Classifier)

How It Works: The Perceptron learns a linear decision boundary by iteratively updating weights and bias whenever a training instance is misclassified, converging to zero errors only if the classes are linearly separable.

Implementation & Role: Implemented from scratch as a linear baseline to test whether demographic age-and-sex feature combinations allow a single hyperplane to separate High and Low mortality risk tiers.

4. Experimental Setup

  • Data Split: For supervised models (KNN and Perceptron), the 920 cleaned records were partitioned using an 80:20 stratified train–test split (737 training observations, 183 testing observations) to preserve class proportions across High and Low Mortality Impact categories. Because K-Means is unsupervised, it was evaluated directly on the unlabeled feature matrix without a train–test split.
  • Hyperparameters Tested:
    • K-Means: Evaluated across candidate cluster counts k (with k = 6 highlighted in Milestone 2; max iterations until convergence at iteration 19).
    • KNN: Tested k \in {3, 5, 7, 9\} using Euclidean distance.
    • Perceptron: Learning rate eta = 0.1, trained for up to 100 epochs.
  • Evaluation Metrics (Implemented From Scratch):
    • Silhouette Score (K-Means): Measures intra-cluster cohesion versus nearest-cluster separation ([-1, 1]) without requiring ground-truth labels.
    • Accuracy, Precision, Recall & Macro F1-Score (KNN & Perceptron): Macro F1 weights High and Low mortality classes equally so majority-class accuracy does not mask minority-class errors.
    • Confusion Matrix (KNN & Perceptron): Breaks down True Positives, True Negatives, False Positives, and False Negatives across risk categories.

6. Discussion

Why Each Method Performed As It Did

The stark performance gap between KNN (Macro F1 = 0.9836) and the Perceptron (Macro F1 = 0.6170)—when interpreted alongside K-Means (Silhouette = 0.7192)—reveals the underlying geometry of regional mortality data:

  • Non-Linearity Defended by K-Means & KNN: K-Means shows that high-burden mortality does not occur along a single linear demographic axis; instead, distinct multi-dimensional pockets emerge (e.g., Cluster 2 driven by infant/under-1 deaths versus Clusters 0 and 5 driven by senior circulatory and metabolic deaths in NCR and nearby regions). Because KNN classifies based on local neighborhood proximity, it adapts naturally to these multiple localized pockets, achieving 0.9836 Macro F1.
  • Why the Perceptron Failed to Converge: A single linear hyperplane cannot simultaneously separate infant-dominated high-risk profiles and senior-dominated high-risk profiles from baseline observations without misclassifying intermediate counts—resulting in persistent training errors (minimum 212 errors across 100 epochs) and high false-positive rates (40 Low-Mortality profiles flagged as High).
  • Neighborhood Size Sensitivity in KNN: Increasing $k$ beyond 5 (to 7 and 9) degraded Macro F1 from 0.9836 to 0.9562 because larger neighborhoods pull in samples from the dense baseline cluster (analogous to K-Means Cluster 1, which contains 760 of 920 records), causing borderline High-Mortality profiles to be outvoted as Low.

Limitations and Possible Improvements

  • Class & Cluster Skew: Baseline low-count cause–region pairs dominate the dataset (760 of 920 records in K-Means Cluster 1), which biases larger neighborhoods in KNN and shifts linear decision boundaries in Perceptron.
  • Raw Counts vs. Population-Normalized Rates: Raw mortality counts favor populous regions such as NCR, Region III, and Region IV-A; normalizing counts per 100,000 regional population would better capture relative epidemiological risk.
  • Future Algorithmic Extensions: Evaluating feature standardization/log-scaling, kernelized or multi-layer perceptrons (MLP), or $k$-fold cross-validation would further test boundary stability.

7. Conclusion

Applying from-scratch K-Means, KNN, and Perceptron models to the 2024 PSA Philippine mortality dataset demonstrates that regional cause-of-death distributions exhibit strong, non-linear demographic structure. K-Means (Silhouette Score = 0.7192) successfully isolated distinct epidemiological subgroups—including infant mortality and NCR-concentrated chronic diseases—while KNN ($k = 5$, Macro F1 = 0.9836) leveraged local feature proximity to classify High and Low mortality risk tiers with high accuracy. By contrast, the Perceptron (Macro F1 = 0.6170) confirmed that these multi-modal demographic mortality profiles are not linearly separable by a single hyperplane.
